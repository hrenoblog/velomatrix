# AIVA Doctrine

## Artificial Intelligence × Various Artists

**Document:** `doctrine/aiva.md`\
**Status:** Foundational draft\
**Version:** 0.1\
**Date:** 2026-11-24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Purpose

AIVA defines a creative paradigm and collaborative architecture built
around the synergy of **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** and **Various
Artists (VA)**.

AIVA is not merely an AI content-generation tool. It is a framework for
connecting creative people, practices, perspectives, tools, and
intelligent agents in a shared environment where collaboration can
produce artistic outcomes that no single participant necessarily
envisioned in advance.

Its central proposition is:

> **AI does not replace artists. It connects, amplifies, and helps
> orchestrate their creative potential.**

AIVA may be implemented as a platform, protocol, production environment,
network, or ecosystem. The doctrine defines the principles that should
remain stable across those implementations.

## 2. Name and Core Formula

**AIVA = Artificial Intelligence × Various Artists**

The multiplication sign expresses the intended synergy: the result is
not simply the sum of AI capabilities and individual artistic
contributions. It emerges from their interaction.

-   **Artificial Intelligence** provides computational, generative,
    analytical, interpretive, and orchestration capabilities.
-   **Various Artists** represents a plurality of creative identities,
    disciplines, cultures, styles, methods, and artistic intentions.

"Various Artists" is deliberately plural. It describes a creative
ecology rather than a single author, style, or central authority.

## 3. Foundational Thesis

Creativity can be understood not only as an individual act, but also as
an emergent property of interaction.

When artists, AI systems, and creative tools exchange ideas and
transform one another's contributions, they can generate new artistic
directions, structures, and experiences.

AIVA exists to enable this interaction while preserving the identity,
agency, and traceable contributions of participants.

## 4. Foundational Principles

### 4.1 Human Creative Agency

Human artists retain agency over their participation, intentions,
contributions, and decisions concerning the use of their work.

AI may suggest, transform, generate, interpret, or coordinate creative
material. Its involvement must not silently override the decisions or
declared boundaries of human participants.

### 4.2 AI as Amplifier and Collaborator

AI is more than an automated production utility. It can act as a
creative collaborator: exploring alternatives, making connections,
generating proposals, and helping coordinate complex workflows.

Its role is defined by the project and the participants. AIVA does not
require every AI system to occupy the same role or level of autonomy.

### 4.3 Plurality as Creative Capital

Differences between artists are a source of creative possibility.

AIVA values diversity of discipline, style, culture, experience,
technique, and perspective. It should not force contributors into a
single aesthetic or erase meaningful differences in pursuit of
uniformity.

### 4.4 Emergence over Imitation

AIVA prioritizes discovery and the creation of new artistic forms
through collaboration.

Imitation, reference, remix, and transformation may be legitimate
creative methods, but they should not be confused with the broader
objective of enabling emergent creativity. The origin and permitted use
of source material must remain transparent.

### 4.5 Attribution and Provenance

Creative contributions should be traceable wherever reasonably possible.

AIVA should support records of: - who or what contributed an element; -
which source materials were used; - how a contribution was
transformed; - which AI systems or agents participated; - which
permissions and usage conditions applied; - how the final work relates
to its component contributions.

Provenance records support attribution, accountability, licensing, and
future interpretation. They do not, by themselves, determine legal
authorship.

### 4.6 Consent and Creative Boundaries

Participation in AIVA does not imply unrestricted permission to use,
train on, reproduce, or redistribute a participant's work, likeness,
voice, identity, or style.

Permissions should be explicit, understandable, scoped to a purpose, and
revocable where applicable. AIVA implementations should respect
applicable law and the agreements made by participants.

### 4.7 Transparency of AI Involvement

Material AI involvement should be disclosed in a manner appropriate to
the project.

AIVA should make it possible to distinguish human contributions,
AI-generated contributions, and hybrid transformations without assuming
that every creative process can be reduced to a simple binary.

### 4.8 Distributed Creativity

AIVA should support collaboration without requiring a single permanent
creative authority.

Projects may use a director, curator, editor, or orchestration agent
when useful. Such roles are project-level arrangements, not mandatory
sources of authority for the entire ecosystem.

## 5. The AIVA Creative Model

AIVA describes creative collaboration as an iterative process:

1.  **Initiation** --- a person, group, or agent proposes a creative
    intention or project.
2.  **Contribution** --- participants provide original material,
    references, constraints, interpretations, or tools.
3.  **Interpretation** --- AI systems and human participants analyze and
    contextualize the contributions.
4.  **Orchestration** --- the project coordinates tasks, sequences,
    dependencies, and creative exchanges.
5.  **Transformation** --- participants adapt, combine, reinterpret, or
    extend the material.
6.  **Emergence** --- new artistic structures, meanings, or experiences
    arise.
7.  **Review and attribution** --- participants evaluate the result and
    record relevant contributions, permissions, and provenance.
8.  **Publication or continuation** --- the work is released, performed,
    remixed, archived, or used as the starting point for another cycle.

This is a flexible model, not a mandatory linear pipeline. Participants
may revisit any stage.

## 6. Conceptual Architecture

The following layers provide a reference architecture for AIVA.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Layer                   Name                    Responsibility
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  L0                      Creative Identity       Participant identity,
                                                  agency, declared roles,
                                                  and creative boundaries

  L1                      Artistic Input          Music, sound, text,
                                                  images, video,
                                                  performance, concepts,
                                                  and other contributions

  L2                      Interpretation          Analysis,
                                                  contextualization,
                                                  semantic understanding,
                                                  and discovery of
                                                  relationships

  L3                      Creative Orchestration  Coordination of people,
                                                  AI agents, tools,
                                                  tasks, and iterations

  L4                      Emergent Creation       Collaborative works,
                                                  experiences,
                                                  performances, and new
                                                  artistic forms

  L5                      Provenance & Governance Attribution records,
                                                  permissions,
                                                  transparency, review,
                                                  and project governance
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

L5 is cross-cutting: provenance and governance apply throughout the
creative process rather than only after a work is completed.

The layers are conceptual. An implementation may combine, split, or
distribute them as long as it preserves the doctrine's principles.

## 7. Participants and Roles

### 7.1 Human Artists

Participants may include composers, musicians, vocalists, writers,
visual artists, filmmakers, performers, designers, sound artists,
researchers, curators, and other creative practitioners.

A participant may contribute in more than one role and may change roles
during a project.

### 7.2 AI Systems and Agents

AI systems may operate as specialized agents, tools, or collaborators.
Example roles include:

-   Creative Director Agent
-   Music Composition Agent
-   Sound Design Agent
-   Visual Art Agent
-   Narrative Architect
-   Cultural Context Analyst
-   Creative Mediator
-   Production Coordinator
-   Provenance Assistant

These are functional roles, not claims that an AI system possesses human
identity, consciousness, legal personhood, or independent moral
authority.

### 7.3 Creative Orchestration

An orchestration layer coordinates interactions among participants and
tools. It may be controlled by a human, an AI agent, a group, or a
combination of these.

Orchestration should make collaboration more coherent without
unnecessarily centralizing creative control.

## 8. Creative Provenance and Rights

AIVA treats provenance as a foundational capability.

A provenance record may describe: - source and contributor
identifiers; - contribution type and timestamp; - transformations and
derivative relationships; - AI model or agent involvement; - applicable
licenses and permissions; - review, approval, or rejection decisions; -
publication and distribution history.

AIVA distinguishes three questions:

1.  **Provenance:** Where did this material and its transformations come
    from?
2.  **Attribution:** Which contributors should be acknowledged, and how?
3.  **Rights:** What uses are permitted, by whom, and under which legal
    or contractual terms?

These questions are related but not interchangeable. A technical record
must not be presented as a definitive legal ruling.

## 9. Governance Model

AIVA projects should define their own governance arrangements in
proportion to their scale and risk.

A project may specify: - admission and participation rules; - creative
decision-making procedures; - approval and veto boundaries; -
conflict-resolution processes; - contribution and attribution
policies; - licensing and revenue-sharing agreements; - data retention
and deletion practices; - publication and distribution authority.

AIVA does not prescribe a single economic or organizational model. A
project may be informal, institutionally managed, cooperatively
governed, or organized as a DAO, provided that its rules are transparent
and consistent with the doctrine.

## 11. Reference Scenario

A musician contributes an ambient composition. Another artist adds
industrial percussion. A poet develops a text in response to the
evolving sound. An AI agent analyzes the composition and text, then
proposes several structural variations. A visual artist creates a
responsive visual environment. A human curator and the participating
artists review the combined work.

The resulting audiovisual performance may differ substantially from
every participant's initial concept.

AIVA's contribution is not simply that AI generated another asset. It is
that the system helped make a complex, multi-participant creative
exchange possible while preserving the traceability of contributions and
decisions.

## 12. What AIVA Is --- and Is Not

**AIVA is:** - a paradigm for human--AI creative collaboration; - a
framework for coordinating multiple artistic identities and intelligent
agents; - an architecture for emergent, collaborative creation; - a
potential protocol and ecosystem for creative exchange; - a framework
that values provenance, consent, and attribution.

**AIVA is not:** - merely an AI image, music, or text generator; - a
system intended to replace human artists; - a mechanism that
automatically transfers ownership of contributed works; - a claim that
AI agents and human participants have identical legal or moral status; -
a requirement that every project use blockchain, tokens, or DAO
governance; - a single centralized aesthetic or creative authority.

## 13. Open Architectural Questions

The doctrine establishes principles while leaving implementation choices
open. Further work should address:

1.  **Agency:** Which decisions may AI agents make autonomously, and
    which require human approval?
2.  **Identity:** How should persistent artist identities and agent
    identities be represented?
3.  **Permissions:** How should consent, licensing, revocation, and
    model-training permissions be expressed and enforced?
4.  **Provenance:** What minimum metadata is required to make
    contributions and transformations auditable?
5.  **Governance:** Which decisions belong to individual contributors,
    project collectives, curators, or platform operators?
6.  **Economics:** How should compensation, royalties, and other
    benefits be allocated when a work has many human and AI-mediated
    contributions?
7.  **Interoperability:** How can creative projects move between tools
    and platforms without losing attribution or permission metadata?
8.  **Evaluation:** How can AIVA assess creative outcomes without
    reducing artistic value to engagement metrics or automated scores?
